A Simple Monthly Content Plan: Publishing 8 Articles a Month Without Burning Out

15 September 20264 min read646 words

The biggest killer of Arabic blogs is not weak writing — it is stopping: one week off becomes a month, then the blog is gone. The fix is not "more motivation" but a smaller, more reliable system. This is how I plan my monthly content, built for one person writing in their spare time.

The principle: 8 articles a month — no more

Two articles weekly is the ideal rhythm for a solo blog: enough for steady activity signals to search engines, realistic alongside a job or study. The plan's value is not in the count but in consistency — eight articles a month for a year beats thirty articles in one month followed by six silent months.

Step 1: the idea tank (always filling, never publishing directly)

Keep a permanent note called "Article ideas" and paste every idea the moment it crosses you: a client question, a comment inquiry, a keyword from your performance report. The four filling sources:

  • Search Console: queries where you appear on pages 2–3 — golden ideas because demand is proven.
  • Customer and visitor questions: every question asked twice deserves an article.
  • Competitors: what do they cover that you do not? (Write it from your own angle.)
  • Daily experience: a problem you solved this week = a practical article with ready material.

Step 2: one planning session per month (90 minutes)

On the last working day of the month, sit for 90 minutes and exit with:

  1. Eight ideas picked from the tank (not the prettiest — the closest to your current audience).
  2. For each idea one line: "This article answers question X for audience Y."
  3. A simple calendar: two articles weekly, with a fixed publish day (e.g., Sunday and Thursday).
  4. One reserve article ready for the worst week.

Step 3: write in batches, not by mood

One hour daily produces less than two weekly three-hour batches — because the mental start-up is the real cost. Try the two-batch system:

  • Research and dump batch: gather sources, outline headings, and dump raw ideas without editing.
  • Shape and publish batch: turn the dump into an article, arrange links and internal links, publish.

Step 4: templates cut half the time

Never start from a blank page. Build a fixed template: an intro with a clear promise (3 lines), 4–6 main headings, a practical summary ending. Ready templates for comparisons, checklists, and how-to guides cut preparation time in half.

Step 5: publishing is not the end — distribution is the other half

For every published article: share it in your channels (Telegram, Facebook, a mailing list if you have one) framed differently per channel, and link to it internally from two related older articles. Internal links lift old and new articles together — the cheapest SEO you can own.

A sample weekly calendar

DayTaskTime
SundayPublish article + share in channels30 min
MondayWriting batch (next week's article)2–3 h
ThursdayPublish article + share + internal linking30 min
FridayFill the idea tank from the week20 min

Total under five hours weekly — a sustainable rhythm that survives months.

What about the bad weeks?

A week with work, illness, or travel will come — this is where the reserve article works, or you publish a short "value roundup" (expanded tips from your archive with commentary). The only rule: never break the weekly rhythm, even if the article gets shorter.

Summary: consistent content needs only three tools: an idea tank that never dries, a monthly planning session, and batch writing. Start this month with eight scheduled articles — after a quarter you will see the difference in both numbers and audience.
